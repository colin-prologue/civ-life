# AgDR-004 — Understandings: knowledge is held by factions, not by civilizations

**Status:** accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Parent intent:** `world-growth-tone`
**Depends on:** `AgDR-003` (the player is a faction)

Named for the inherited knowledge in Adrian Tchaikovsky's *Children of Time*,
which is the reference this borrows its shape from: knowledge carried by
lineages, transmitted imperfectly, sometimes lost.

## Decision

Technology and practice are **Understandings** — discrete bodies of knowledge
held by *factions*, not by civilizations and not by the player as an
out-of-world score.

Three properties follow, and they are the whole design.

### 1. Sharing and holding are both real moves

| | Gain | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Share** an Understanding | influence within the polity; the whole advances | you lose distinctiveness — you are less singular |
| **Hold** it | you stay singular and necessary | the whole advances slower; you risk irrelevance rather than indispensability |

This is the central recurring decision of the political layer, and it is a
genuine dilemma rather than a resource cost — both options are defensible in the
same position.

### 2. Held knowledge is portable; shared knowledge is not

**When a faction splinters, it takes what it held. What it shared stays
behind.**

So hoarding buys mobility and sharing buys standing. One tension serves both the
political layer and the faction-mobility loop in `AgDR-003`, which is why this is
worth building around rather than bolting on.

It also gives the splinter its character. A splintering faction arrives
materially tiny but carrying practices the surrounding world has not reached —
refugees with knowledge. The uphill climb is completely real in every material
sense, and the advantage is not a difficulty slider that will get tuned into
irrelevance.

### 3. Transmission is imperfect, so history is not a ratchet

An Understanding held by a faction that is scattered, absorbed, or diminished
**can be lost from the world entirely.** The world's level of advancement can go
down as well as up.

This is the most valuable property here. It makes **dark ages possible without
being punitive** — they are not aimed at the player, they are just what happened
— and it turns the era system from a climb into a history. A tech system that
only ratchets upward has no story in it.

It also makes the player's movement through the world *generative*: splintering
scatters knowledge into new places, so the mechanism by which a world advances
across ages is substantially the player moving through it and seeding what they
carry.

## World-level advancement

Ages turn when **the world** is ready, not when the player wins a race. Every
faction contributes; knowledge spreads between them; the era clock reads the
world's aggregate state.

This is what makes eras compatible with a non-competitive design, and it is why
the player returning in a later age finds a world carrying the advancement of
everyone who lived through the earlier ones — including their own former selves.

## What was rejected

**A conventional per-civilization tech tree.** Simpler, familiar, and every
player already knows how to read one. Rejected because it makes advancement a
race even when nothing is racing, it cannot express knowledge being lost, and it
has no way to represent a small faction that knows something a large one does
not — which is the specific situation this design keeps producing.

**Knowledge as a player-level meta-progression.** Rejected for the same reason
`AgDR-003` rejects a bloodline: it would make the player an entity outside the
world rather than a participant in it, and the persistence should belong to the
world, not to a save file.

## What would make this wrong

**If the share/hold decision has an obviously correct answer.** If sharing is
almost always right, this is a formality with extra steps; if holding is, the
polity never advances and the political layer is inert. The dilemma has to stay
genuinely balanced across a range of positions, and that is a tuning question
this record cannot settle.

**If losing knowledge feels like punishment rather than history.** The intent
file forbids punitive design. If a player experiences a lost Understanding as
something taken from *them* rather than something that happened to the world,
property 3 is in conflict with the intent file and the intent file wins.

Early signal: the first time an Understanding is lost while the player is
watching. Whether that reads as poignant or as unfair decides this.
